Operations

How the map, the stops and the marks work

The floor plan is the golem's own map, released into its journal. Click a room or press a place to add a stop, then send the golem through the stops in that order (Visit) or let it choose the order that makes the whole road shortest (Cover). Between stops it always takes the shortest road through the doors and open boundaries, journals it, and journals every passage crossed and every stop reached. The world (Gazebo, the kiosk) holds more than the map knows: what the body bumps into there becomes a red mark on this map (g.Bump, told to the peers, who g.LearnMark it); marks close together are joined as the vertices of one obstacle, the figure of the thing nobody charted. The golem feels for a way past, right then left, and if none fits its body it decides the road again around the marks. Only when no road fits does the mission fail.

    Let go: every pending mission is abandoned — one command, journaled as g.Abandon(id, reason) per mission, nothing erased — and the body is put back on its mark. Reset everything (lab lever): wipes the journals of this golem AND its peers and reboots them reborn at entry 1 — all of them, because the hearer dedups the sender's tell ids.

    Journal — live

      Everything the journal receives, record by record, as it lands: the release chain, each define action (the template), each action (the template with its arguments), the tells, the acks and the verdicts. The tag at the right is the record kind. At boot the whole diary is replayed here first.

      Obstacles

      What the golem HYPOTHESIZES from what its bodies touched — one query on /obstacles walking g.Obstacles(). A thing is geometry the roads avoid: its vertices are the marks that outlined it, each with the normal of the touch. A peer is a body it met — history, never planned around. The marks are facts; the figure is the guess, and every new touch refines it.

      Runtime events

        Ephemeral facts (roads chosen, legs started, holds, membrane, lifecycle) — they never touch the journal.

        Why a query must stay read-only

        Queries read the golem's in-memory state and never touch the journal. Keep them read-only: a mutating call here would change memory without journaling it, and the change evaporates on the next rehydration. Distances follow the map's passages: g.Distance(map.Find('kitchen'), map.Find('garage')) is the shortest road through the doors, and the header's route left · eta comes from /progress, which only adds the road from where the body stands.
