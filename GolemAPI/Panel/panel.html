Operations

How the line, the map and the marks work

The buttons compose the command line at the top of the page; nothing runs until you press run or Enter. Click a room, press a place or add the pair to put a point on the line; visit sends the golem through the points in that order, cover lets it choose the order that makes the whole road shortest, dash goes in that order with the doors crossed on the way (fewer stops, ajuste 58), then tells one more stop to the route underway. Between stops it always takes the shortest road through the doors and open boundaries, journals it, and journals every passage crossed and every stop reached. The world (Gazebo, the kiosk) holds more than the map knows: what the body bumps into there becomes a red mark on this map (g.Bump, told to the peers, who g.HearBump it and mark it too); marks close together are joined as the vertices of one obstacle, the figure of the thing nobody charted. The language is the robot's own, like a shell's — visit 2,9.5 9,8, reset --all, set stops 2,9.5 9,8 then visit @stops — never the actor's scripts (/commands lists it: the table below). Add --with blue (or --with all, or press a peer under the line) to involve more than one golem: this golem does its part and carries the same command to each peer named; the answers come back per golem.

Commands — the language, one line each; click an example to put it on the line

pause / resume: the golem is held where its body stands — journaled as g.Pause(me), the body stops, the plan and the cursor keep — and let go on with g.Resume(me). reset: every pending route is abandoned — journaled as route.Abandon(reason), nothing erased — and the body is put back on its mark. reset --all (lab lever): wipes the journals of this golem AND its peers and reboots them reborn at entry 1. set / show are the console's: a value kept in this browser, written as @name.

Journal — live

    Everything the journal receives, record by record, as it lands: the release chain, each define action (the template), each action (the template with its arguments), the tells, the acks and the verdicts. The tag at the right is the record kind. At boot the whole diary is replayed here first.

    Obstacles

    What the golem HYPOTHESIZES from what its bodies touched — one query on /obstacles walking collisions.All(). A thing is geometry the roads avoid: its vertices are the marks that outlined it, each with the normal of the touch. A peer is a body it met — history, never planned around. The marks are facts; the figure is the guess, and every new touch refines it.

    Runtime events

      Ephemeral facts (roads chosen, legs started, holds, membrane, lifecycle) — they never touch the journal.
